Style guide.

Everything the site is made of: colors, type, marks, spacing, components, and the rules for the Divi parts you build yourself. The plugin's own stylesheet renders this page, so what you see here is what the site does.

Principles

The box score. Flat and ruled. Hairline lines and whitespace carry structure. No shadows, no gradients, corners at 6px or less. Dense where the data is, airy where the words are.

Brand palette, light only. Chalk paper, navy, red. The logo's colors are the site's colors. Field green exists only inside the charts. There is no dark theme.

Team identity comes from marks, not logos. Every team is drawn as a jersey tile or a pennant generated from its two colors and its abbreviation. Never a league or team logo, never a helmet, never a wordmark.

Type does the talking. Tourney for page titles only. Barlow Condensed for everything uppercase and small. IBM Plex Sans for sentences. IBM Plex Mono for numbers in tables and labels that read like data.

Copy is casual and direct. Short sentences. Team nicknames as plural nouns ("Lions allow", "Vikings take a sack"). No em dashes, no en dashes.

Team marks

Generated from a team's primary and secondary colors plus its abbreviation. The text inside is the secondary color if it reads at 3:1 on the primary, else white, else near-black. No files to manage; the plugin draws them in PHP and JavaScript from the same rules.

Jersey tile, the working mark

Sizes: 18px in table column heads, 22px in the game picker, 24px in standings and strip cards, 28px in slate rows, 34px in the Game of the Week and The game boxes. Always with the abbreviation or name beside it; the tile never stands alone.

Pennant, the hero mark

Only three places: next to the team name on the explorer title (48px tall), as a facing pair above the matchup title (40px), and in the team picker's "pick up where you left off" box (40px). Nowhere smaller than 40px.

All 32 tiles

DoTreat the mark as the team's identity in the UI. Where a mark is present, no other team color appears in that row or card.
Don'tUse real logos, helmets or wordmarks anywhere, including social images and the sidebar.

Layout and spacing

Sidebar column232px wide, sticky, paper background, 1px line on the right. Built in Divi (see below).
Content columnFills the rest. Max width 1240px. Padding 22px top, 16 to 32px sides, 56px bottom.
Top bar12px vertical padding, hairline below. Breadcrumb left in Plex 13px, "Updated" stamp right in Mono 11px.
Vertical rhythm18px between panels. 10px between title and first panel. 8px padding inside the strip.
PanelPanel background, 1px line, 6px radius. Header row 10px 14px with a hairline below. Body 12px 14px. Tables sit 14px in from the panel edge.
RowsHairline between rows. 7 to 9px vertical padding. Hover: Panel 2 background. Whole row is a link.
Radius6px panels and buttons, 4px tiles, badges and chart corners, 2px bars. Nothing rounder.
BreakpointsThe app sizes itself by its own column width (container queries): two columns above 1000px, single column below; titles stack below 820px; tables shed columns below 600px.

Motion

From transitions.dev, pasted not reinvented: titles rise in with a 40ms stagger; the filter pill and view tabs slide; stat tile digits pop in when a value changes; the detail badge swaps text; tab views slide in with a cross-blur; the play tooltip fades and scales in after 80ms and vanishes instantly; the score strip lifts 2px on hover with a soft return. Everything respects reduced motion. Nothing bounces.

Other pages (About, Privacy, anything else)

Use the same layout. Keep the body in IBM Plex Sans 15 to 16px on paper, headings in Barlow Condensed uppercase, one Tourney page title at the top with a red kicker above it. Max text width 70ch. Links underlined in red. The site has exactly one tone; an About page written in the app's voice is the goal.

Voice

Do"Lions give up 100 yards a game to tight ends, most in the league." Plural nicknames, plain numbers, the rank in parentheses.
Don't"The Detroit Lions' defensive unit has struggled to contain opposing tight ends this season." No hedging, no filler, no full city names in copy.
DoHeadings that sound like a sideline: "Tight ends feast", "Pressure problem", "Run it at them".
Don'tEm dashes, en dashes, exclamation points, betting language ("lock", "fade", "smash").
